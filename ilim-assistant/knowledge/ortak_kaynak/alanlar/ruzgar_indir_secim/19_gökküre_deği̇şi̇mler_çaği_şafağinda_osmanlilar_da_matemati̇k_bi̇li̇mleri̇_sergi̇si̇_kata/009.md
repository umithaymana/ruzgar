33 GÖKKÜRE MATHEMATICAL SCIENCES IN THE OTTOMAN REALM AT THE THRESHOLD OF THE AGE OF TRANSFORMATION Mūḍiḥ al-Awqāt fī Maʿrifat al-Muqanṭarāt / موضح الأوقات في معرفة المقنطرات An Arabic treatise on the rubʿ al-muqanṭarāt dedicated to Sultan Bāyezīd II by the Ottoman astronomer Muḥammad Qunawī, one of the scholars who shaped the development of the science of timekeeping in the Ottoman Empire. Arabic / Naskh 1a–30a ff ., 11 ll. 175x105-125x50 mm. Th is treatise, included within a mecmūʿa (miscellany), is written in Naskh script on chickpea-colored, sized Eastern paper using gold, black, and red ink. Th e paper is of heterogeneous texture. Th e second work in the mecmūʿa consists of a draft copy of the same treatise, apparently reproduced in the same scribal hand. Space has been left for illumination in the manuscript, but no decoration was executed. On folio 1r, there is a waqf statement bearing the ṭughrā of Sultan Mahmud I. On folio 27v, there is a drawing executed in red ink. Th e explanatory notes added over the drawing are written in black ink in Naskh script. Th e binding consists of the front and back covers, a sertāb (fore-edge fl ap) and a miqlab (envelope fl ap). Th e exterior of the binding is covered in dark brown leather. Th e covers and the envelope fl ap feature shamsa (medallion) with rūmī ornamentation executed in blind tooling. Th e shamsa is surrounded, and the edges of the covers are framed, with gilt fillets. Muḥammad b. Kātib Sinān al-Qūnawī al-Muwaqqit (d. after 930/1524) was educated in the scholarly milieu established in Istanbul by ʿAlī Qūshjī and the circle of mathematicians and astronomers around him. His inheritance of both the tradition of the Samarqand mathematical-astronomical school and the classical Islamic science of timekeeping, which reached its peak in 14th-century Damascus through Shams al-Dīn al-Khalīlī and Ibn al-Shāṭir, the development of timekeeping studies in the Ottoman Empire. Among these treatises, his work titled Mūḍiḥ al-Awqāt, written in Arabic in twenty-five chapters, concerns the Rubʿ al-Muqanṭarāt and was dedicated to Sultan Bāyezīd II. Th e Rubʿ al-Muqanṭarāt is a practical instrument that represents all the circles and arcs on the front face and rete of the astrolabe on a quarter-circle. Th is small and portable instrument can be used by anyone with basic knowledge of astronomy and a user manual. For this reason, it became especially well known among astronomers engaged in timekeeping studies. TÜYEK, Süleymaniye Manuscript Library, Ayasofya, 2708

34 GÖKKÜRE DEĞİŞİMLER ÇAĞI ŞAFAĞINDA OSMANLILAR’DA MATEMATİK BİLİMLERİ Mîzânü’l-Kevâkib / ميزان الكواكب Mîkât ilminin Osmanlı’daki gelişimine yön veren astronomi âlimlerinden Muhammed Konevî’nin Kânûnî Sultan Süleyman’a sunduğu Arapça astronomi eseri. Arapça / Nesih 266 yk., 15 st. 259x147-180x100 mm. Eser krem renkli aharlı Doğu kâğıdı üzerine siyah ve kırmızı renk mürekkep kullanılarak nesih hatla yazılıdır. Tablo ve çizimler kırmızı renktedir. Kâğıt heterojen yapıdadır. Reddâde kullanılan metinde satır araları altın duraklıdır. 1b sayfasında kitabe formunda unvan tezyinatı bulunmaktadır. Bezemeli alanın lacivert renk zemin üzerine rûmî ve hatâyî grubu motifler ile süslenmiştir. T ezyinat lacivert renkte tığ ile bitirilmiştir. Nüshanın 1a sayfasında Sultan I. Mahmûd tuğralı vakıf mührü yer almaktadır. Mîzânu’l-kevâkib Muhammed b. Kâtib Sinan el- Konevî el-Muvakkit’in (ö. 930/1524 sonrası) en önemli eserlerindendir. Yıldızların konumuna dayalı olarak namaz vakitlerini gösteren cetvellerden müteşekkil bu eser, telif edildiği tarihe kadar Güneş’i değil yıldızları esas alan ilk kapsamlı çalışma olması bakımından, Osmanlıların mîkât ilmine en özgün katkısı kabul edilir. Eser, 266 varakta yaklaşık 250.000 sayısal veri içerir. Konevî, bu eseri hiçbir gözlem aleti kullanmaksızın vakit tayini yapabilmek amacıyla telif ettiğini bildirir. Nitekim 41 derece İstanbul enlemine göre hazırlanan cetveller, yıl boyunca her gün 500’den fazla yıldızın güney 0 derecesine ulaştığı andaki dört vakti bildirir. Konevî’nin tartışmasız başyapıtı sayılabilecek Mîzânu’l-kevâkib’i, gözlem aleti ihtiyacını ortadan kaldıran niteliği ve zîclerdeki sayısal verilerden yararlanılarak hazırlanmış olması bakımından diğer mîkât cetvellerinden ayrılır. TÜYEK, Süleymaniye YEK, Ayasofya 2710

35 GÖKKÜRE MATHEMATICAL SCIENCES IN THE OTTOMAN REALM AT THE THRESHOLD OF THE AGE OF TRANSFORMATION Mīzān al-Kawākib / ميزان الكواكب An Arabic astronomical work presented to Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent by Muḥammad Qunawī, one of the astronomers who shaped the development of timekeeping science in the Ottoman Empire. Arabic / Naskh 266 ff., 15 ll. 259x147-180x100 mm. The work is written in Naskh script on cream- colored, sized Eastern paper, using black and red ink. Tables and drawings are in red. The paper has a heterogeneous texture. In the text, where reddāde (catchword) is used, the spaces between lines are marked with gold verse marker. On folio 1v, there is an illuminated headpiece in the form of a cartouche. The decorated area is ornamented with Rūmī and Khaṭā’ī motifs on a dark blue background. The illumination is finished with a dark blue tīgh (vertical stalks) outline. On folio 1r of the manuscript, there is a waqf statement bearing the ṭughrā of Sultan Maḥmūd I. Mīzān al-Kawākib is among the most important works of Muḥammad b. Kātib Sinān al-Qūnawī al- Muwaqqit (d. after 930/1524). Consisting of tables that determine prayer times based on the positions of the stars, this work is regarded as the most original contribution of the Ottomans to timekeeping science, as it represents the first comprehensive study up to its time to take the stars rather than the Sun as its basis. The work contains approximately 250,000 numerical data across 266 folios. Qūnawī states that he composed this work to determine time without using any observational instruments. Indeed, the tables prepared for the latitude of Istanbul at 41 degrees indicate the four prayer times throughout the year based on the moments when more than 500 stars each day reach 0 degrees in the southern meridian. Considered Qūnawī’s undisputed masterpiece, Mīzān al-Kawākib differs from other timekeeping tables in that it eliminates the need for observational instruments and is prepared using numerical data derived from astronomical tables (zījes). TÜYEK, Süleymaniye Manuscript Library, Ayasofya 2710

Arapça / Talik 37 yk., 15 st. 180x127-114x58 mm. Sultan II. Bayezid’in saray kütüphanesinde bulunan nüsha, krem renk aharlı Doğu kâğıdı üzerine siyah ve kırmızı mürekkep kullanılarak talik hattıyla yazılmıştır. Eserin cildi kahverengi deridir. Ön-arka kapaklar ve mıklebinde soğuk baskı tekniğinde mekik formlu şemse bezemelidir. Salbekler zencerek motifi şeklinde yapılmıştır. Kapak ve mıklep kenarları zencerek motifi ve cetvellerle çevrelenmiştir. T ezyin edilmeyen nüshanın zahriye sayfasında Sultan II. Bayezid’in mührü ve Hayreddin Hızır Atûfî’nin saray kütüphanesi kaydı bulunmaktadır. Yine zahriyede Ayasofya Kütüphanesi’nin banisi Sultan I. Mahmûd’un vakıf mührü ile dönemin Haremeyn evkafı müfettişi Şeyhzâde Ahmed’in vakıf kaydı ve şahsî mührü yer almaktadır. Nüshanın son sayfasında Sultan II. Bayezid’in mührü vardır. Baba tarafından Kadızâde Rûmî’nin, anne tarafından Ali Kuşçu’nun torunu olan Kutbuddin Çelebi’nin gökkuşağı, renklerin oluşumu ve Ay ile Güneş çevresinde görünen hâle fenomenlerini optik ve doğa felsefesi açılarından ele aldığı bu eseri, konu üzerine yazılmış en geniş metinlerden biridir. Aristoteles, İbn Sînâ, İbnü’l-Heysem, Fahreddin Râzî ve Nasîruddin T ûsî gibi isimlerin renk oluşumu ve görme olayına dair teorilerini değerlendirerek bu fenomenlere dönemin bilimsel paradigması içinde bir izah getirmeye çalışmıştır. Metin içinde yer yer dedesi ve hocası Ali Kuşçu’nun T ecrîd şerhine atıfl ar yer almaktadır. Başka bir koleksiyonda mevcut kendi müsveddesinden hareketle kabiliyetli bir hattata istinsah ettirdiği ve son satırlarını kendi kalemiyle tamamladığı bu nüsha Sultan II. Bayezid’e arz edilen nüsha olmalıdır. Sayfa derkenarlarında “minhu” ile işaret edilen ve eserin müellifi Kutbuddin Çelebi’ye ait olan pek çok ek açıklama da bulunmaktadır. 37 GÖKKÜRE MATHEMATICAL SCIENCES IN THE OTTOMAN REALM AT THE THRESHOLD OF THE AGE OF TRANSFORMATION Risāla fī Qaws Quzaḥ wa’l-Hāla / رسالة في قوس قزح والهالة A copy of the work in which Quṭb al-Dīn Çelebi, the grandson of ʿAlī Qūshjī and Qāḍīzāde al-Rūmī, deals with halo phenomena formed around rainbows, colors, and celestial bodies. Arabic / Taʿlīq 37 ff ., 15 ll. 180x127-114x58 mm. Th e copy located in the palace library of Sultan Bāyezīd II is written in Taʿlīq script in black and red ink on cream-colored, sized Eastern paper. Th e binding of the work is brown leather. Th e front and back covers and the miqlab (envelope fl ap) are decorated with a stamped recessed technique featuring a shuttle-shaped shamsa (medallion). Th e salbek (pendant) are executed in an zencerek (interlaced chain borders). Th e edges of the covers and miqlab are bordered with zencerek and ruling lines. Th e manuscript, which is not illuminated, contains on its front fl yleaf the seal of Sultan Bāyezīd II and the palace library record of Khayr al-Dīn Khiḍr ʿAṭūfī. Also on the fl yleaf are the waqf statement of Sultan Maḥmūd I, the founder of the Ayasofya Library, as well as the waqf statement and personal seal of Shaykhzāde Aḥmad, the inspector of the al-Ḥaramayn endowments of the period. On the final page of the manuscript, there is a statement of Sultan Bāyezīd II. A work by Quṭb al-Dīn Çelebi, grandson on his father’s side of Qāḍīzāde al-Rūmī and on his mother’s side of ʿAlī Qushjī, in which he examines rainbow, the formation of colors, and halo phenomena observed around the Moon and the Sun from the perspectives of optics and natural philosophy, and which is one of the most extensive texts written on the subject. By evaluating the theories on the formation of colors and vision of figures such as Aristotle, Ibn Sīnā, Ibn al-Haytham, Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, and Naṣīr al-Dīn Ṭūsī, he attempts to provide an explanation for these phenomena within the scientific paradigm of his time. Within the text, there are occasional references to his grandfather and teacher ʿAlī Qushjī’s commentary on the Tajrīd. Based on his own draft preserved in another collection, this copy was transcribed by a skilled calligrapher and its final lines were completed in his own hand, it is likely the copy presented to Sultan Bāyezīd II. Th e margins of the pages also contain numerous additional notes marked with “minhu,” belonging to the author Quṭb al-Dīn Çelebi. TÜYEK, Süleymaniye Manuscript Library, Ayasofya 2414
