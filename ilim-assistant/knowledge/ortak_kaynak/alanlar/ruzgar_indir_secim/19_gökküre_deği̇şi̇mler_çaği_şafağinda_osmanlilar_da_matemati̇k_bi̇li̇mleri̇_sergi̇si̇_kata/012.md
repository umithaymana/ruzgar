Ali el-Muvakkit’in rubu‘ tahtasının yapımı, özellikleri ve kullanımına dair Türkçe risalesinin nüshası. Türkçe / Talik 35b-49b yk. 29 st. 197x135-153x70 mm. Eser koyu nohudi renk aharlı ve filigranlı Avrupa kâğıdı üzerine siyah ve kırmızı mürekkep kullanılarak nesih hatla yazılıdır. Geniş süzgeç izleri sırta dik ve heterojen yapıdadır. Sekiz eserlik bir mecmuanın üçüncü eseri olan risalede tezyinat bulunmamaktadır. 1a sayfasında Sultan I. Mahmûd tuğralı vakıf mührü yer alan nüsha reddâdeli olup, başlıklar kırmızı mürekkeple yazılmıştır. Nüshanın cildi, ön ve arka kapaktan oluşmaktadır. Ciltte sertab ve mıklep bağlantılarının izleri görülmesine rağmen mıklep günümüze ulaşmamıştır. Cilt, açık kahverengi deri üzerine uygulanmış pembe ve yeşil renklerden oluşan gelgit ebru ile kaplanmış olup ebru cilt özelliği göstermektedir. XVI. yüzyıl Osmanlı astronomi bilgini Muslihuddin Mustafa b. Ali el-Muvakkit er-Rûmî halk arasında daha çok Koca Saatçi lakabıyla tanınmıştır. Uzun süre Sultan Selim Camii’nde muvakkitlik yaptığı için Selîmî nisbesiyle anılmış, 967/1560 yılından itibaren yürüttüğü Müneccimbaşı vazifesine vefatından sonra Takiyyuddin Râsıd tayin edilmiştir. Müellif, 935/1529 tarihinde Türkçe yazdığı bu eserini, rub‘u’l-müceyyeb yapımı, özellikleri ve kullanımı üzerine kaleme almıştır. Bir mukaddime ile yirmi beş bab olarak telif edilen eserin kütüphanelerde 5 farklı versiyona ait 100’e yakın nüshası vardır. Bu durum, müellifin zaman içerisinde çalışmasını tekrar ele alıp yeni tecrübelerini eklediğini göstermektedir. TÜYEK, Nuruosmaniye YEK, Nuruosmaniye 2947

51 GÖKKÜRE MATHEMATICAL SCIENCES IN THE OTTOMAN REALM AT THE THRESHOLD OF THE AGE OF TRANSFORMATION Tashīl al-Mīqāt / تسهيل الميقات A manuscript copy of the T urkish treatise on the construction, properties, and use of the rubu‘ tahtası (quadrant) by Chief astrologer Mustafa b. ʿAlī al- Muwaqqit, the predecessor of Taqī al-Dīn Rāṣid. T urkish / Taʿlīq 35b–49b ff., 29 ll. 197x135-153x70 mm. The manuscript is written in Naskh script in black and red inks on dark chickpea-coloured, sized and watermarked European paper. The laid lines are wide, perpendicular to the spine, and the paper exhibits a heterogeneous texture. The treatise forms the third item in a mecmūʿa (miscellany) consisting of eight works and contains no decoration. Folio 1r bears a waqf statement with the ṭuġrā of Sultan Maḥmūd I. The text has reddāde (catchwords), and headings are in red ink. The binding consists of the upper and lower covers. Although traces of the sertāb (fore-edge flap) and miqlab (envelope flap) connections are visible, the flap itself has not survived. The binding is covered with marbled paper in pink and green tones applied over light brown leather, representing an example of a marbled binding. The sixteenth-century Ottoman astronomer Muṣliḥ al- Dīn Muṣṭafā b. ʿAlī al-Muwaqqit al-Rūmī (d. 959/1571) was commonly known among the public by the nickname Koca Saatçi (the Great Timekeeper). Having served for a long period as muwaqqit (timekeeper) at the Sultan Selim Mosque, he was also known by the nisba Selīmī. From 967/1560 onwards, he held the office of müneccimbaşı (chief astrologer) and after his death, Taqī al-Dīn Rāṣid was appointed to this position. The author composed this T urkish work in 935/1529 on the construction, properties, and use of the rubʿ al-mujayyab. The work, arranged in a muqaddima (introduction) and twenty-five bāb (chapters) has nearly one hundred manuscript copies representing five different versions preserved in libraries. This indicates that the author revised the work overtime and added his experiences to it. TÜYEK, Nuruosmaniye Manuscript Library, Nuruosmaniye 2947

Abī al-Fatḥ al-Ṣūfī. Arabic, naskh 25 ff., 33 ll. 28x19-20x13 mm. The manuscript is written in naskh script in black and red ink on light chickpea-colored, sized European paper bearing watermarks. No illumination has been employed, all rulings and headings are executed in red ink. On folio 1r appears the statement of Ḥāfiẓ Efendi, the owner of the collection. The manuscript’s binding consists of a front and a back cover. The spine is covered with dark brown leather. The covers are made of thin cardboard and decorated with battal marbling in blue, pink, and yellow colors. As emphasized in Sidrat al-muntahā al-afkār by Taqī al-Dīn Rāṣid, this work was composed with the aim of revising the Zīj-i Ulugh Beg. Originally written in Persian, the Zīj-i Ulugh Beg was translated into Arabic by Yaḥyā b. ʿAlī al-Rifāʿī at the request of Ibn Abī al-Fatḥ. In his work, designed in four maqāla (book). Ibn Abī al-Fatḥ recalculated the astronomical tables originally prepared according to the longitude of Samarqand in accordance with the longitude of Egypt. The presence of a copy of the work in the private library of the English astronomer John Greaves (d. 1652) suggests that the work also circulated in Europe. 57 GÖKKÜRE MATHEMATICAL SCIENCES IN THE OTTOMAN REALM AT THE THRESHOLD OF THE AGE OF TRANSFORMATION Dustūr al-ʿAmal wa-Taṣḥīḥ al-Jadwal / دستور العمل و تصحيح الجدول A copy of the Persian commentary written on Zīj-i Ulugh Beg by Mīrim Chelebi, one of the most prominent mathematician-astronomers of the Ottoman period after Qāḍīzāda al-Rūmī and ʿAlī Qūshjī. Persian / Naskh 109 fols., 23 lines 250×175–185×125 mm. The manuscript is written in naskh script with black and red ink on cream-colored burnished Eastern paper. Headings, as well as tables and diagrams, are marked in red ink. The seal of Shaykh al-Islām Veliyyüddin Efendi, the owner of the collection, appears on folio 1a and on several other pages throughout the manuscript. The binding consists of front and back covers, a sertāb (fore- edge flap), and a miklep (fore-edge flap). The binding is covered with a khatīb ebru composed of black, pink, brown, yellow, and green hues applied over dark brown leather, and it exemplifies the marbled-binding tradition. The author, a grandson of both Qāḍīzāda al-Rūmī and ʿAlī Qūshjī, served as a mudarris (professor) at the madrasas of Gelibolu, Edirne, Bursa, and Istanbul. After establishing himself as an authority in mathematics and astronomy, he was invited to the palace, where he instructed Bayezid II in the mathematical sciences; he subsequently served as Anatolian kāżīʿaskar under Selim I. This work is a Persian-language commentary on the Zīj-i Ulugh Beg, which was itself composed in Persian. Completed in 904/1499 by order of Sultan Bayezid II, the work draws on al-Kāshī’s Zīj-i Khāqānī and on ʿAlī Qūshjī’s earlier Sharḥ-i Zīj-i Ulugh Beg. Mīrim Çelebi adopts a didactic approach and demonstrates five distinct methods, with worked examples, for calculating the sine of an arc of one degree. The work also reveals his particular interest in the values of trigonometric expressions, in which he arrives at original results. TÜYEK, Beyazıt Manuscript Library, Veliyyüddin 2276
