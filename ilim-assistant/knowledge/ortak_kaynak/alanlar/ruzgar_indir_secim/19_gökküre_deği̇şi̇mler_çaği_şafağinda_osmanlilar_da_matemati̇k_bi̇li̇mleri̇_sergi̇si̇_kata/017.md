81 GÖKKÜRE MATHEMATICAL SCIENCES IN THE OTTOMAN REALM AT THE THRESHOLD OF THE AGE OF TRANSFORMATION T erceme-i T ezkiretü’l-Küttāb fī ʿIlm al-Ḥisāb / ترجمهٔ تذكرة الكتاب في علم الحساب An illuminated copy of the T urkish translation-commentary of the mathematical work T ezkiretü’l-küttāb fī ʿilm al-ḥisāb by the mathematician, astronomer, and physician Ghars al-Dīn Ibn al-Naqīb, prepared with additions by his student Dervīş Meḥmed b. Luṭfī. T urkish / Naskh 146 fols., 13 lines 143x222 mm Th e work is written in naskh script with gold, black, and red ink on chickpea-colored burnished Eastern paper. Th e copy is framed with gold ruling throughout. Folio 1b bears a title decoration composed of rūmī and khatāʾī group motifs in gold, blue, green, white, and red on gold and blue grounds. Seals appear at the beginning and end of the manuscript. Th e binding consists of front and back covers; traces of a sertāb (fore-edge fl ap) and miqlab (envelope fl ap) are visible on the binding, though these elements have not survived and are not presently extant. Th e exterior of the binding is covered in dark brown leather. Th e covers feature a shamsa (medallion) and salbek (pendant) executed in polychrome and leather-filigree technique, incorporating a composition of cloud, khatāʾī, and rūmī motifs. Th e edges of the covers are framed with gold ruling. Th e mathematical work T ezkiretü’l-küttāb fī ʿilm al-ḥisāb by Ghars al-Dīn Ibn al-Naqīb was translated in 982/1574 with additional material by the author’s student Dervīş Meḥmed b. Luṭfī, and dedicated to Sokollu Meḥmed Pasha, grand vizier of Selim II. Th e work consists of an introduction, two bāb (chapter), and a hātima (conclusion).Th e introduction comprises two sections: the first concerns the names, types, and place values of numbers, and the second treats the forms and writing of numerals. Th e first chapter covers arithmetic operations with whole numbers — addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, extraction of roots, and verification. Th e second chapter deals with fractions and operations performed with them. Th e concluding section, which constitutes one third of the treatise, is headed “proportional numbers.” Here the author emphasizes geometric ratio and divides the conclusion into three faṣl (section): the first treats the definition and use of four proportional numbers; the second covers commercial transactions and various methods of applying addition, subtraction, and multiplication to problems, as well as the place of four proportional numbers in algebraic problems; and the final subsection addresses the concept of profit and various related problems. TÜYEK, Köprülü Manuscript Library, Fazıl Ahmed Paşa 936

82 GÖKKÜRE DEĞİŞİMLER ÇAĞI ŞAFAĞINDA OSMANLILAR’DA MATEMATİK BİLİMLERİ Umdetü’l-Hisâb / عمدة الحساب Matematik bilginliğinin yanı sıra hat ve minyatür sanatçısı kimliği ile de bilinen Matrakçı Nasûh’un Türkçe matematik kitabının bezemeli nüshası. Türkçe / Nesih 177 yk., 15 st. 215x128-144x72 mm. Eser krem renkli aharlı Doğu kâğıdı üzerine altın ve siyah mürekkep kullanılarak nesih hatla yazılıdır. Zahriyede, etrafı dendanla çevrili lacivert madalyon üzerine altın mürekkeple eser ve müellif ismi yazılıdır. 1b sayfasında unvan bezemesi yer almaktadır. Bezeme altın ve lacivert zemin üzerine çift tahrir tekniğinde hatâyî grubu motifler ile oluşturulmuştur. 1b-2a sayfaları altın cetvelli, geri kalan sayfalar ise kırmızı cetvellidir. Başlıklar, tablo ve cetveller altın mürekkeple oluşturulmuştur. Nüshanın cildi ön-arka kapak, sertab ve mıklepten oluşmaktadır. Cildin dışı koyu mor deri ile kaplıdır. Kapaklarda ve mıklepte rûmî kompozisyon içeren soğuk baskı tekniğinde şemse ve salbekler bulunmaktadır. Kapakların ve mıklebin kenarları zencerek ile çevrelenmiştir. Enderun mektebinde yetişen ve matematik bilginliğinin yanı sıra hat ve minyatür sanatçısı kimliği ile de öne çıkan Matrakçı Nasuh el-Bosnevî, Cemâlü’l-küttâb ve kemâlü’l-hüssâb’ı kaleme alarak Yavuz Sultan Selim’e ithaf etmiş, bir süre sonra eserini genişleterek Umdetü’l- hisâb’ı yazmıştır. Kânûnî Sultan Süleyman’a ithaf edilen Umdetü’l-hisâb, Osmanlı muhasebe matematiğinin seyrini açık şekilde ortaya koyan özgün eserlerdendir. İki kısımdan meydana gelen eserin birinci kısmı yirmi iki fasla ayrılırken, ikinci kısımda farklı elli problemin çözümü örnek olarak verilmektedir. Şemalarla düzenlenmiş problem çözümleri Umdetü’l-hisâb’ın özgün yönlerinden biridir. Müellif Matrakçı Nasûh, bu şemalar yardımıyla, metinde sunulan problem çözümünün anlaşılmasında okuyucuya kolaylık sağlamayı amaçlamıştır. TÜYEK, Süleymaniye YEK, Şehid Ali Paşa 1987

83 GÖKKÜRE MATHEMATICAL SCIENCES IN THE OTTOMAN REALM AT THE THRESHOLD OF THE AGE OF TRANSFORMATION ʿUmdat al-Ḥisāb / عمدة الحساب An illuminated copy of T urkish mathematic book by Matrakçı Naṣūḥ, known as a calligrapher and miniaturist in addition to his mathematical scholarship. T urkish / Naskh 177 fols., 15 lines. 215x128-144x72 mm. The work is written in naskh script using gold and black ink on cream-colored sized (ahar) Eastern paper. On the ẓahriyya, the title of the work and the author’s name are written in gold ink on a dark blue shamsa (medallion) surrounded by dendān (scallops). An illuminated ʿunwān (title piece) is located on folio 1b. The illumination is composed of khaṭāyī group motifs in the double tahrir (double-contour) technique on a gold and dark blue ground. Folios 1b-2a have gold ruling, while the remaining folios have red ruling. Headings, tables, and rulings are executed in gold ink. The binding of the manuscript consists of front and back covers, a sertab (fore-edge flap), and a miqlab (envelope flap). The exterior of the binding is covered in dark purple leather. The covers and the miqlab feature central shamsas and salbeks (pendants) containing rūmī compositions applied using a blind-tooling technique. The edges of the covers and miqlab are bordered with a zencerek (interlaced chain border). Matrakçı Naṣūḥ al-Busnawī, who was trained in the Enderūn school and stood out with his identity as a calligrapher and miniaturist as well as his mathematical scholarship, authored Jamāl al-kuttāb wa-kamāl al-ḥussāb and dedicated it to Sultan Selīm I; a short time later, he expanded his work and wrote ʿUmdat al-ḥisāb. Dedicated to Sultan Süleymān the Magnificent, ʿUmdat al-ḥisāb is among the original works that clearly reveal the course of Ottoman accounting mathematics. While the first part of the two-part work is divided into twenty-two chapters, the solutions to fifty different problems are provided as examples in the second part. Problem solutions arranged with diagrams constitute one of the original aspects of ʿUmdat al-ḥisāb. With the help of these diagrams, the author Matrakçı Naṣūḥ aimed to facilitate the reader’s understanding of the problem solutions presented in the text. TÜYEK, Süleymaniye Manuscript Library, Şehid Ali Paşa 1987
