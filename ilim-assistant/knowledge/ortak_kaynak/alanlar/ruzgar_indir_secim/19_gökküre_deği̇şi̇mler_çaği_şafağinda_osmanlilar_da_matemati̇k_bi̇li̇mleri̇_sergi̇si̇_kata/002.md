Known in Ottoman history as the “Magnificent Century” and in T urkish historiography as the “T urkish Century,” this era cannot be adequately understood solely through the lens of wars, conquests, and struggles between states. Indeed, the changes in the traditional perception of science, culture, art, and especially nature during this century were at least as significant as the political developments of the period. The Ottomans were a world empire that ruled over a vast geography and possessed a powerful bureaucracy, a sophisticated legal system, and a conception of science and art nourished by well-established educational institutions and a comprehensive vision of civilization. As the principal centers for the training of the scholarly class (‘ilmiyye), the madrasas provided instruction not only in the religious sciences but also in fields such as logic, philosophy, mathematics, astronomy, and medicine. Owing to the contributions of all Ottoman sultans -and particularly to Sultan Mehmed II’s exceptional policy of attracting leading scholars, scientists, technologists, artists, intellectuals, and men of letters to the imperial capital- İstanbul had become an important center of learning from the fifteenth century onward. By preserving and advancing the classical Islamic scientific tradition, the Ottomans continued to produce original works and innovations in such fields as mathematics, astronomy, calendrical calculations, observational instruments, and medicine. In Europe, meanwhile, knowledge gradually began to move beyond the monopoly of the clergy and reached wider segments of society. The Renaissance, the reformation, and the geographical discoveries started to transform the traditional worldview; new living spaces, new trade routes, and new observations prompted the questioning of established assumptions and, together with the influence of the accumulated legacy of Islamic science, accelerated developments in cartography, mechanics, and astronomy. Taqī al-Dīn al-Rāṣid occupies a prominent place among the mathematicians, mechanicians, and astronomers produced by the Ottoman scientific world in the sixteenth century and is widely regarded as the last great representative of classical Islamic astronomy. Major centers of learning such as Damascus, Cairo, and Istanbul played a decisive role in shaping his scientific personality and intellectual development. His appointment as chief astronomer (müneccimbaşı) by Sultan Selim II in 1571 proved to be a turning point not only in his own career but also in the history of Ottoman science. This position enabled Taqī al-Dīn al- Rāṣid to establish closer ties with the political and scholarly circles of Istanbul and ultimately led to the foundation of the Istanbul Observatory by decree of Sultan Murad III in 1579. Established on the hills of T opkhāna, the observatory was conceived under Taqī al-Dīn al-Rāṣid’s leadership as a major scientific institution. It housed a library containing important works and instruments in astronomy and related sciences and also functioned as a center of education where the leading specialists of the era gathered and collaborated. T ophane sırtlarında kurulan, ilgili bilimlere dair önemli eser ve aletleri ihtiva eden bir kütüphane ve dönemin uzmanlarının toplandığı bir eğitim kurumu işlevi de gören bu rasathane, Takiyyuddin Râsıd’ın öncülüğünde teşekkül etmiştir. Takiyyuddin Râsıd’ın hayatından bahsetmek, sadece bürokratik görevler üstlenen bir müderrisin değil, aynı zamanda astronomik gözlemler yapan, mekanik/robotik aletler geliştiren, hesap yöntemlerini yenileyen ve farklı bilim geleneklerini birleştiren bir bilim adamının yaşam hikâyesine; dolayısıyla bir dönemin bilim anlayışına, politikalarına ve mecrasına ışık tutmaktır. 2026 yılı, bu büyük Osmanlı âliminin doğumunun (14 Haziran 1526) 500. yıl dönümüdür. Bir medeniyetin büyüklüğünün en önemli göstergelerinden biri, yetiştirdiği insanlardır. Bir diğer göstergesi ise bu insanlara sahip çıkma, onları anlama, anlatma ve onların mirasını yaşatma kabiliyetidir. Yıl dönümleri de bu tür çalışmalar için gelenekleşen önemli bir vesiledir. Takiyyuddin Râsıd’ın -farklı tezahürleri olmasına rağmen- günümüz için en önemli başarılarından birisi olduğuna inandığımız husus, İslâm-Türk bilim anlayışının yalnızca geçmiş bilgileri aktaran durağan bir yapıdan ibaret olmadığını; yeni teori, yöntem, teknik ve araçlar geliştiren dinamik bir karakter sergilediğini ispatlayan çalışmalar yapması ve bu dinamizmin devam edebileceğine işaret etmesidir. Bu nedenledir ki “Gökküre: Değişimler Çağı Şafağında Osmanlılar’da Matematik Bilimleri” sergimizle Takiyyuddin Râsıd’ın çalışmalarını gündeme getirirken, içinde yer aldığı döneme de bütüncül bir yaklaşımla, mensubu olduğu “Türk Asrı”nın birikimlerine dikkat çekmek istedik. Sergi ve katalog kitabı, iki bölümden oluşmaktadır. Birincisi ve odak noktası, yazma eserlerden ibarettir. İkinci bölüm, teorilerin icraya yansımasını da gösteren aletlerden oluşmaktadır. Yazma eserlerin also held important bureaucratic positions, but also the story of a scientist who conducted astronomical observations, developed mechanical instruments and self-operating devices, introduced innovative computational methods, and brought together diverse scientific traditions. His life provides valuable insight into the scientific outlook, policies, and intellectual environment of an entire era. The year 2026 marks the 500th anniversary of the birth (14 June 1526) of this great Ottoman scholar. One of the most important indicators of the greatness of a civilization is the people it produces. Another one is its ability to appreciate, understand, present, and preserve the legacy of those individuals. Anniversaries have long served as important occasions for undertaking such efforts. Despite the many different dimensions of his legacy, one of Taqī al-Dīn al-Rāṣid’s most significant achievements for the present day, in our view, is that his work demonstrates that the Islamic-T urkish scientific tradition was not merely a static structure devoted to transmitting inherited knowledge. Rather, it possessed a dynamic character capable of generating new theories, methods, techniques, and instruments, while also pointing to the possibility of the continuation of such intellectual vitality. For this reason, through our exhibition titled “Gökküre: Mathematical Sciences in the Ottoman Realm at the Threshold of the Age of T ransformation” we sought to bring Taqī al-Dīn al-Rāṣid’s achievements back into public view, as well as to draw attention to the rich intellectual and scientific heritage of the “T urkish Century” to which he belonged. The exhibition and its accompanying catalogue are composed of two sections. The first -and principal- section consists of manuscripts, while the second features scientific instruments that demonstrate the practical application of theoretical knowledge. At the center of the manuscript section are the works of Taqī al-Dīn al-Rāṣid.
