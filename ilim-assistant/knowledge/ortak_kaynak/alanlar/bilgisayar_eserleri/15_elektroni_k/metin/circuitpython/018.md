Supply voltage range: 4.5 to 5.5 volts Basic CMOS Digital Logic Gate One of the main disadvantages with the TTL digital logic gate series is that the logic gates are based on bipolar transistor logic technology and as transistors are current operated devices, they consume large amounts of power from a fixed +5 volt power supply. Also, TTL bipolar transistor gates have a limited operating speed when switching from an &ldquo;OFF&rdquo; state to an &ldquo;ON&rdquo; state and vice-versa called the &ldquo;gate&rdquo; or &ldquo;propagation delay&rdquo;. To overcome these limitations complementary MOS called &ldquo;CMOS&rdquo; ( C omplementary M etal O xide S emiconductor) logic gates which use &ldquo;Field Effect Transistors&rdquo; or FET&rsquo;s were developed. As these gates use both P-channel and N-channel MOSFET&rsquo;s as their input device, at quiescent conditions with no switching, the power consumption of CMOS gates is almost zero, (1 to 2&mu;A) making them ideal for use in low-power battery circuits and with switching speeds upwards of 100MHz for use in high frequency timing and computer circuits. 2-input NAND Gate This basic CMOS gate example contains three N-channel normally-off enhancement MOSFET&rsquo;s, one for each input consisting of FET 1 and FET 2 , and an additional switching MOSFET, FET 3 which is biased permantly &ldquo;ON&rdquo; through its gate. When one or both inputs &ldquo;A&rdquo; and &ldquo;B&rdquo; are grounded to logic level &ldquo;0&rdquo;, the corresponding input MOSFET, FET 1 or FET 2 are switched &ldquo;OFF&rdquo; producing a logic &ldquo;1&rdquo; (HIGH) output condition from the source terminal of FET 3 . Only when both inputs &ldquo;A&rdquo; and &ldquo;B&rdquo; are held HIGH at logic level &ldquo;1&rdquo;, does current flow through the corresponding MOSFET switching it &ldquo;ON&rdquo; producing an output state at Q equivalent to a logic level &ldquo;0&rdquo; as both MOSFETS, FET 1 and FET 2 are conducting. Therefore producing the switching action representative of a NAND gate function. Improvements in the circuit design with regards to switching speed, low power consumption and improved propagation delays has resulted in the standard CMOS 4000 &ldquo;CD&rdquo; family of logic IC&rsquo;s being developed that complement the TTL range. As with the standard TTL digital logic gates, all the major digital logic gates and devices are available in the CMOS package such as the CD4011, a Quad 2-input NAND gate, or the CD4001, a Quad 2-input NOR gate along with all their sub-families. Like TTL logic, complementary MOS (CMOS) circuits take advantage of the fact that both N-channel and P-channel devices can be fabricated together on the same substrate material to form various logic functions. One of the main disadvantage with the CMOS range of IC&rsquo;s compared to their equivalent TTL types is that they are easily damaged by static electricity. Also unlike TTL logic gates that operate on single +5V voltages for both their input and output levels, CMOS digital logic gates operate on a single supply voltage of between +3 and +18 volts. Common CMOS Sub-families include: &bull;&nbsp;4000B Series: Standard CMOS &nbsp;&ndash;&nbsp;These devices are the original Buffered CMOS family of logic gates introduced in the early 70&rsquo;s and operate from a supply voltage of 3.0 to 18v d.c. &bull;&nbsp;74C Series: 5v CMOS &nbsp;&ndash;&nbsp;These devices are pin-compatible with standard 5v TTL devices as their logic switching is implemented in CMOS but with TTL-compatible inputs. They operate from a supply voltage of 3.0 to 18v d.c. Note that CMOS logic gates and devices are static sensitive, so always take the appropriate precautions of working on antistatic mats or grounded workbenches, wearing an antistatic wristband and not removing a part from its antistatic packaging until required.
