Another reason to use capacitive coupling rather than direc t is its relative lack of signal
attenuation. Direct coupling through a resistor has the dis advantage of diminishing, or atten-
uating, the input signal so that only a fraction of it reaches the base of the transistor . In many
applications, some attenuation is necessary anyway to prev ent signal levels from “overdriving”
the transistor into cutoff and saturation, so any attenuati on inherent to the coupling network
is useful anyway . However , some applications require that t here be no signal loss from the in-
put connection to the transistor’s base for maximum voltage gain, and a direct coupling scheme
with a voltage divider for bias simply won’t sufﬁce.
So far , we’ve discussed a couple of methods for coupling an input signal to an ampliﬁer , but
haven’t addressed the issue of coupling an ampliﬁer’s output to a load. The example circuit
used to illustrate input coupling will serve well to illustr ate the issues involved with output
coupling.
