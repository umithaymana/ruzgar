3.5 Inductor quirks
In an ideal case, an inductor acts as a purely reactive device . That is, its opposition to AC
current is strictly based on inductive reaction to changes i n current, and not electron friction as
is the case with resistive components. However , inductors are not quite so pure in their reactive
behavior . To begin with, they’re made of wire, and we know tha t all wire possesses some
measurable amount of resistance (unless its superconducti ng wire). This built-in resistance
acts as though it were connected in series with the perfect in ductance of the coil, like this:
(Figure 3.15)
Consequently , the impedance of any real inductor will alway s be a complex combination of
resistance and inductive reactance.
Compounding this problem is something called the skin effect , which is AC’s tendency to
ﬂow through the outer areas of a conductor’s cross-section r ather than through the middle.
