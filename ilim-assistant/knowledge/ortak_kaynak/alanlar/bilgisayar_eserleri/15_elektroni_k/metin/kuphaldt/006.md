When Benjamin Franklin made his conjecture regarding the di rection of charge ﬂow (from
the smooth wax to the rough wool), he set a precedent for elect rical notation that exists to
this day , despite the fact that we know electrons are the cons tituent units of charge, and that
they are displaced from the wool to the wax – not from the wax to the wool – when those
two substances are rubbed together . This is why electrons ar e said to have a negative charge:
because Franklin assumed electric charge moved in the oppos ite direction that it actually does,
and so objects he called ”negative” (representing a deﬁcien cy of charge) actually have a surplus
of electrons.
By the time the true direction of electron ﬂow was discovered , the nomenclature of ”posi-
tive” and ”negative” had already been so well established in the scientiﬁc community that no
effort was made to change it, although calling electrons ”po sitive” would make more sense in
