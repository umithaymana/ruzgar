Typically,Newton’smethodisusedtofindrootsfairlyquickly.However,thingscangowrong.SomereasonswhyNewton’s

Example 4.48
When Newton’s Method Fails
Considerthefunction f (x) = x3 − 2x + 2. Let x0 = 0. Show that the sequencex1, x2 ,… fails to approach a
root of f .
Solution
For f (x) = x3 − 2x + 2, the derivative is f ′ (x) = 3x2 − 2. Therefore,
x1 = x0 − f (x0)
f ′ (x0) = 0 − f (0)
f ′ (0) = − 2
−2 = 1.
In the next step,
x2 = x1 − f (x1)
f ′(x1) = 1 − f (1)
f ′ (1) = 1 −1
1 = 0.
Consequently, the numbersx0, x1, x2 ,… continue to bounce back and forth between0 and 1 and never get
closer to the root off which is over the interval[−2, −1] (seeFigure 4.81). Fortunately, if we choose an
initial approximation x0 closer to the actual root, we can avoid this situation.
