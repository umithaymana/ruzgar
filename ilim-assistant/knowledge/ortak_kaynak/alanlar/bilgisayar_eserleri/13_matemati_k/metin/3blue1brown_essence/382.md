Example 2.58 Chapter Opener: Finding the Focus of a Parabolic Reflector Energy hitting the surface of a parabolic reflector is concentrated at the focal point of the reflector ( Figure 2.86 ). If the surface of a parabolic reflector is described by equation x 2 100 + y 2 100 = z 4 , x 2 100 + y 2 100 = z 4 , where is the focal point of the reflector? Figure 2.86 Energy reflects off of the parabolic reflector and is collected at the focal point. (credit: modification of CGP Grey, Wikimedia Commons) Solution Since z is the first-power variable, the axis of the reflector corresponds to the z -axis. The coefficients of x 2 x 2 and y 2 y 2 are equal, so the cross-section of the paraboloid perpendicular to the z -axis is a circle. We can consider a trace in the xz -plane or the yz -plane; the result is the same. Setting y = 0 , y = 0 , the trace is a parabola opening up along the z -axis, with standard equation x 2 = 4 p z , x 2 = 4 p z , where p p is the focal length of the parabola. In this case, this equation becomes x 2 = 100 · z 4 = 4 p z x 2 = 100 · z 4 = 4 p z or 25 = 4 p . 25 = 4 p . So p is 6.25 6.25 m, which tells us that the focus of the paraboloid is 6.25 6.25 m up the axis from the vertex. Because the vertex of this surface is the origin, the focal point is ( 0 , 0 , 6.25 ) . ( 0 , 0 , 6.25 ) .
