The FAIR Guiding Principles Below are listed the FAIR Guiding Principles [1] as a reference for guiding FAIR implementation. They provide guidance when improving Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability and Reusability of digital resources. For machine-actionable reference, the principles are also defined in our ontology&nbsp; here .&nbsp; ​ ​ See also the interpretation of the FAIR Guiding Principles by the GO FAIR community. Findable Button ​The first step in (re)using data is to find them. Metadata and data should be easy to find for both humans and computers. Machine-readable metadata are essential for automatic discovery of datasets and services, so this is an essential component of the FAIRification process. F1 (meta)data are assigned a globally unique and persistent identifier GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation F2 data are described with rich metadata GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation F3 metadata clearly and explicitly include the identifier of the data it describes GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation F4 (meta)data are registered or indexed in a searchable resource GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation Accessible Button Once the user finds the required data, she/he/they need to know how they can be accessed, possibly including authentication and authorisation. A1 (meta)data are retrievable by their identifier using a standardized communications protocol GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation A1.1 the protocol is open, free, and universally implementable GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation A1.2 the protocol allows for an authentication and authorization procedure, where necessary GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation A2 metadata are accessible, even when the data are no longer available GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation Interoperable Button The data usually need to be integrated with other data. In addition, the data need to interoperate with applications or workflows for analysis, storage, and processing. I1 (meta)data use a formal, accessible, shared, and broadly applicable language for knowledge representation GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation I2 (meta)data use vocabularies that follow FAIR principles GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation I3 (meta)data include qualified references to other (meta)data GO FAIR Foundation Interpretation Reusable Button The ultimate goal of FAIR is to optimise the reuse of data. To achieve this, metadata and data should be well-described so that they can be replicated and/or combined in different settings.
