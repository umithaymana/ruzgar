When working out problems that have a uniform distribution, be careful to note if the data is inclusive or exclusive of endpoints. We will assume that the smiling times, in seconds, follow a uniform distribution between zero and 23 seconds, inclusive. This means that any smiling time from zero to and including 23 seconds is equally likely . The histogram that could be constructed from the sample is an empirical distribution that closely matches the theoretical uniform distribution. Let X = length, in seconds, of an eight-week-old baby's smile. The notation for the uniform distribution is X ~ U ( a , b ) where a = the lowest value of x and b = the highest value of x . The probability density function is f ( x ) = 1 b − a 1 b − a for a ≤ x ≤ b . For this example, x ~ U (0, 23) and f ( x ) = 1 23 − 0 1 23 − 0 for 0 ≤ X ≤ 23. Notice that the theoretical mean and standard deviation are close to the sample mean and standard deviation in this example.
