[ 267 ] In 2003 the Poincaré conjecture was solved by Grigori Perelman (who declined to accept an award, as he was critical of the mathematics establishment). [ 268 ] Most mathematical journals now have online versions as well as print versions, and many online-only journals are launched. [ 269 ] [ 270 ] There is an increasing drive toward open access publishing , first made popular by arXiv . Many other important problems have been solved in this century. Examples include the Green–Tao theorem (2004), existence of bounded gaps between arbitrarily large primes (2013), and the modularity theorem (2001). The AKS primality test was published in 2002, which is the first algorithm that can determine whether a number is prime or composite in polynomial time . A proof of Goldbach's weak conjecture was published by Harald Helfgott in 2013; as of 2025, the proof has not yet been fully reviewed. The first einstein was discovered in 2023. In addition, a lot of work has been done toward long-lasting projects which began in the twentieth century. For example, the classification of finite simple groups was completed in 2008. Similarly, work on the Langlands program has progressed significantly, and there have been proofs of the fundamental lemma (2008), as well as a proposed proof of the geometric Langlands correspondence in 2024. Future [ edit ] Main article: Future of mathematics The future trajectory of mathematics is anticipated to be significantly influenced by the integration of advanced computational methodologies, though the exact epistemological consequences remain a subject of ongoing academic debate. [ 271 ] The utilization of artificial intelligence (AI) and automated theorem provers, such as Rocq and Lean , is increasingly prominent in the formal verification of complex theorems. While proponents suggest this integration may enhance logical rigor, some scholars argue it could potentially obscure fundamental human comprehension if proofs become too computationally dense for independent human verification. [ 272 ] Furthermore, the application of machine learning algorithms to identify heuristic patterns in fields like knot theory is hypothesized to accelerate mathematical discovery; however, the reliance on algorithmic processes necessitates cautious epistemological scrutiny regarding verifiability. [ 273 ] In the realm of applied mathematics, the theoretical development of quantum computing is widely projected to necessitate a paradigm shift in cryptography . Algorithms such as Shor's algorithm possess the theoretical capacity to efficiently factorize large prime numbers, which would ostensibly compromise widely used public-key cryptosystems like RSA . [ 274 ] Consequently, researchers are actively developing post-quantum cryptography , an area heavily reliant on algebraic geometry and lattice-based structures. Nevertheless, the timeline for the practical engineering of scalable, fault-tolerant quantum computers remains highly uncertain, making the immediate urgency of this cryptographic transition a topic of theoretical speculation.
