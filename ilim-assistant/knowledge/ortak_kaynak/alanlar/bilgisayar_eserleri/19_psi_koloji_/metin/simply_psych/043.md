Radical feminists emphasize that oppression occurs in both public (work, politics) and private (family, relationships) spheres. Key Takeaways Root Cause: Radical feminists treat patriarchy, a system of male domination, as the root cause of women&#8217;s oppression in both public and private life. Two Origins: Firestone traced patriarchy to biology and childbearing; Millett located it in socialisation and culture. Most radical feminists now draw on both. Rape as Control: Brownmiller (1975) argued rape functions as “a conscious process of intimidation by which all men keep all women in a state of fear.” Key Criticisms: Multicultural, Marxist, and postmodern feminists argue radical feminism overstates a universal “sisterhood” and understates race, class, and history. Modern Evidence: A 2022 global study estimated that 27% of ever-partnered women aged 15–49 have experienced physical or sexual partner violence in their lifetime. Patriarchy as the Root Cause of Oppression The word ‘radical’ means ‘of or relating to the root.’ Radical feminists believe gender inequality is rooted in men’s need to control women. They see patriarchy as a system, not the prejudice of a few individuals. This branch of feminism therefore argues that gradual reform cannot work. Equality requires overthrowing the patriarchal order itself. Most radical feminists treat patriarchy as a domination of females by males that recurs across historical periods and cultures. It is not tied to one economic system. They differ on its exact basis, but most agree it involves men’s appropriation of women’s bodies and sexuality in one of three ways: Reproduction: Control of reproduction, including whether women can access abortion and contraception. The family: The nuclear family keeps women dependent through unpaid domestic labour. Violence: Rape, domestic abuse, and sexual exploitation maintain male control. Radical feminists pursue systemic change and activism, not legislative or economic reform. The goal is total liberation. They theorize new ways to think about the relationships between men and women so that women can be liberated. They see women as a collective group that has been, and still is, oppressed by men. Its intent is women-centered. Women’s experiences and interests sit at the forefront of its theory and practice. It is argued by some to be the only theory genuinely by and for women (Rowland &amp; Klein, 1996). Theoretical Roots of Patriarchy Radical feminists have never agreed on a single explanation for why patriarchy exists. Two founding texts set out different accounts, and the disagreement between them still shapes the tradition today. Firestone: Biology and Reproduction Shulamith Firestone was one of the earliest and most influential radical feminist theorists. In The Dialectic of Sex (1970), she located the origin of patriarchy in biology rather than economics or culture. This class system, she argued, could only be abolished by eliminating the biological basis of dependency. She controversially advocated artificial reproduction. In her view, it was a technological route to liberation. Millett: Socialisation and Politics Her focus was different. In Sexual Politics (1970), developed from her doctoral work, she analysed literature and psychoanalytic theory, including a sustained critique of Freud. Millett argued that patriarchy operates as a political institution. On her reading, it is sustained not primarily by biology but by the socialisation of women into subordinate roles from childhood onward. The effects reached everywhere. Sexual relationships are themselves relationships of power, Millett argued. Literature and psychology had, for generations, naturalised male dominance by presenting it as an inevitable consequence of anatomy rather than a political arrangement that could be challenged. Where Firestone grounded patriarchy in the body, Millett grounded it in ideology and culture. This distinction shapes later radical feminist writing. The two accounts are not mutually exclusive, and most radical feminist writing after the 1970s draws on both. But the disagreement over which mechanism is primary is one reason radical feminism is better understood as a family of related arguments than a single unified theory. What Are The Principles Of Radical Feminism? Below are some of the key areas of focus which are essential to understanding radical feminism: Patriarchal institutions Radical feminists believe that there are existing political, social, and other institutions that are inherently tied to the patriarchy. Three institutions stand out: Government laws: Laws have restricted what women can do with their bodies. The church: It has long confined women to the maternal role and rejected non-reproductive sexuality. Marriage: Radical feminists define traditional marriage as a patriarchal institution since it makes women part of men’s private property. Even today, marriage can be seen as an institution perpetuating inequalities through unpaid domestic work, most of which is still done by women. Control over women’s bodies According to radical feminists, patriarchal systems attempt to gain control over women’s bodies. Patriarchal institutions control the laws of reproduction where they determine whether women have the right to an abortion and contraception. Thus, women have less autonomy over their own bodies. Kathleen Barry stated in her book Female Sexual Slavery (1979) that women in marriage are seen to be ‘owned’ by their husband. She also suggested that women’s bodies are used in advertising and pornography alike for the male use. Women are objectified From a radical feminist standpoint, the patriarchy, societal sexism, sexual violence, and sex work all contribute to the objectification of women. They accuse pornography of objectifying and degrading women. It displays unequal male-female power relations. With prostitution, radical feminists argue that it trivializes rape in return for payment and that prostitutes are sexually exploited. The struggle against pornography has come to occupy such a central position in the radical feminist critique of male supremacist relations of power. Campaigns against it are intended to expose how men are willingly trained to view and objectify women (Thompson, 2001). Violence against women Radical feminists believe that women experience violence by men physically and sexually, but also through prostitution and pornography. They believe that violence is a way for men to gain control, dominate, and perpetuate women’s subordination. According to radical feminists, violence against women is not down to a few perpetrators. It is a wider, societal problem. They claim there is a rape culture that is enabled and encouraged by a patriarchal society. Transgender disagreement There is disagreement about transgender identity in the radical feminist community. While some radical feminists support the rights of transgender people, others oppose including transgender women in the category “woman.” Trans Exclusionary Radical Feminists, or TERFs, are one such group.
