To find a clinical trial near you, search&nbsp; clinicaltrials.gov &nbsp; , a database of privately and publicly funded clinical studies. Other ways to find a clinical trial Talk to your health care provider about studies that may be right for you. You can also learn about studies in newspapers, TV, or online. Join a national registry of research volunteers , such as ResearchMatch&nbsp; . ResearchMatch is a nonprofit program funded by NIH that helps connect people interested in research studies with researchers from medical centers across the United States. Join the NIH All of Us &nbsp;Research Program &nbsp; ,&nbsp; which is enrolling a large group of people that reflects the diversity of the United States. The program aims to build a diverse database that can inform thousands of studies on various health conditions. How do I sign up to participate in a clinical trial? After you find a clinical trial you're interested in, contact the study team to learn more about it. You can usually find the study teams&rsquo; contact information in the trial&rsquo;s description. The staff can give you information that will help you decide whether to participate. Check out this resource from the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) for a list of specific questions to ask about volunteering for a research study&nbsp; . Let your health care provider know if you decide to join a clinical trial. They may want to talk to the study team to help coordinate your care and ensure the trial is safe for you. The meeting featured three presentations from National Institutes of Health (NIH) staff who spoke about ongoing mental health research initiatives, a brief discussion panel with these presenters, and an hour-long question-and-answer session with Dr. Joshua Gordon, Director of NIMH, outlining NIMH&rsquo;s new outreach approach to increase public access to mental health research, and to facilitate communication between NIMH, professional societies, and advocacy groups. 2019 Meeting Highlights &nbsp; There were no meetings in 2019. 2018 Meeting Highlights &nbsp; Alliance for Research Progress October 19, 2018, Bethesda, MD Featured presentations and discussion focused on Prediction and Prevention of Psychosis in At Risk Youth, Evaluation of the National Zero Suicide Model in Real-World Clinical Settings, the Role of Cognitive Neuroscience in Alleviating Neuropsychiatric Symptom, and an NIMH Alliance Engagement Session. Professional Coalition for Research Progress March 20, 2018, Rockville, MD The National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) convened the ninth meeting of the Professional Coalition for Research Progress (Coalition).
