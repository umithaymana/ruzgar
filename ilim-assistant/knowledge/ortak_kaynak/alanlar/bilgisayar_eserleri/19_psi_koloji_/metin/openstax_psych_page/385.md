Prolonged grief disorder is a new addition to the DSM-5-TR, based on recommendations, analysis, and review by experts and researchers. Studies have shown that despite overlaps with other disorders—including depressive disorder and posttraumatic stress disorder—PGD is unique and and is associated with significant suffering and enduring functional impairments (Golden &amp; Dalgliesh, 2010; Spuij et al 2012). PDG is associated with increased risk for serious diseases, including cardiovascular conditions and immune system deficiency (Prigerson et al, 1997). Once a new diagnosis is added to the DSM, its evidence-based improvement continues. For example, the criteria and description for PGD specifically references the loss of a person. Recent studies have focused on grief resulting from the loss of a pet, and have generally found that a small but significant portion of people do experience impairment mirroring that of PGD (Highland, 2026; Lee, 2019). As a result, some researchers and practitioners indicate that the definition of PGD should be expanded.
