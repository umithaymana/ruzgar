Serotonin dysfunction, as noted earlier, is also known to play an important role in depression; low levels of serotonin have also been linked to aggression and impulsivity (Stanley et al., 2000). The combination of these three characteristics constitutes a potential formula for suicide—especially violent suicide. A classic study conducted during the 1970s found that patients with major depressive disorder who had very low levels of serotonin attempted suicide more frequently and more violently than did patients with higher levels (Asberg, Thorén, Träskman, Bertilsson, &amp; Ringberger, 1976; Mann, 2003). Any indications of self harm, as well as suicidal thoughts, plans, and even off-hand remarks (“I might kill myself this afternoon”) should always be taken extremely seriously. People who hurt themselves or contemplate terminating their life need immediate help. Below is a link to an excellent website that contains resources (including hotlines) for people who are struggling with suicidal ideation or self harm, have loved ones who may be suicidal, or who have lost loved ones to suicide: http://www.afsp.org . In the United States, you can also contact the 24-hour National Suicide Prevention Hotline by calling 988 or texting HOME to 741741 to access their Crisis Text Line. The Crisis Text line also has help for other issues and problems that people may encounter. Other nations may have similar services; if you do not know about them, you could visit the International Association for Suicide Prevention site, which includes support information and also has a search tool for finding support in other countries.
