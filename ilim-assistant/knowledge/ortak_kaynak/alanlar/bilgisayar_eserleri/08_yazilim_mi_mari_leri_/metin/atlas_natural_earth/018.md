In archeology [ edit ] GIS has been an important tool in archaeology since the early 1990s. [ 116 ] Early users of GIS in archaeology predominately used it for predictive modelling to identify potential archaeological sites. [ 117 ] As the use of GIS within archaeology has become more widespread it is now used for: The creation of highly detailed maps of archaeological excavations, surveys and historical sites. Identification of spatial patterns, using spatial analysis , between artefacts and other archaeological sites. These patterns can be used to infer past human behaviours e.g. resource use, settlement patterns and past mobility. [ 118 ] [ 31 ] 3D visualisations of historic sites. GIS is used to increase the efficiency and precision of field research. [ 31 ] In cultural heritage conservation and management [ edit ] GIS is also used to help manage the conservation of cultural heritage sites. GIS helps conservation organizations monitor the impacts of development, conflict, and climate change on archaeological and other cultural resources. [ 119 ] Some public agencies use GIS software to assess the potential impacts of construction and other development and use these assessments in permitting and mitigation processes.
