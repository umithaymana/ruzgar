Unexpected Reply from an Unknown Delegate When an &quot;Attendee&quot; delegates an item to another CU, they MUST send a &quot;REPLY&quot; method to the &quot;Organizer&quot; using the &quot;ATTENDEE&quot; properties to indicate that the request was delegated and to whom. Hence, it is possible for an &quot;Organizer&quot; to receive a &quot;REPLY&quot; from a CU not listed as one of the original &quot;Attendees&quot;. The resolution is left to the implementation, but it is expected that the calendaring software will either accept the reply or hold it until the related &quot;REPLY&quot; method is received from the &quot;Delegator&quot;. If the version of the &quot;REPLY&quot; method is out of date, the &quot;Organizer&quot; SHOULD treat the message as a &quot;REFRESH&quot; message and update the &quot;Delegate&quot; with the correct version, provided that delegation to that delegate is acceptable. 5.3 . Security Considerations iTIP is an abstract transport protocol that will be bound to a real- time transport, a store-and-forward transport, and perhaps other transports. The transport protocol will be responsible for providing facilities for authentication and encryption using standard Internet mechanisms that are mutually understood between the sender and receiver. 6.1 . Security Threats 6.1.1 . Spoofing the Organizer In iTIP, the &quot;Organizer&quot; (or someone working on the &quot;Organizer&#x27;s&quot; behalf) is the only person authorized to make changes to an existing &quot;VEVENT&quot;, &quot;VTODO&quot;, or &quot;VJOURNAL&quot; calendar component and republish it or redistribute updates to the &quot;Attendees&quot;. An iCalendar object that maliciously changes or cancels an existing &quot;VEVENT&quot;, &quot;VTODO&quot;, or &quot;VJOURNAL&quot; calendar component may be constructed by someone other than the &quot;Organizer&quot; and republished or sent to the &quot;Attendees&quot;. 6.1.2 . Spoofing the Attendee In iTIP, an &quot;Attendee&quot; of a &quot;VEVENT&quot; or &quot;VTODO&quot; calendar component (or someone working on the &quot;Attendee&#x27;s&quot; behalf) is the only person authorized to update any parameter associated with their &quot;ATTENDEE&quot; Daboo Standards Track [Page 124] RFC 5546 iTIP December 2009 property and send it to the &quot;Organizer&quot;. An iCalendar object that maliciously changes the &quot;ATTENDEE&quot; parameters may be constructed by someone other than the real &quot;Attendee&quot; and sent to the &quot;Organizer&quot;. 6.1.3 . Unauthorized Replacement of the Organizer There will be circumstances when &quot;Attendees&quot; of an event or to-do decide, using out-of-band mechanisms, that the &quot;Organizer&quot; must be replaced. When the new &quot;Organizer&quot; sends out the updated &quot;VEVENT&quot; or &quot;VTODO&quot;, the &quot;Attendee&#x27;s&quot; CUA will detect that the &quot;Organizer&quot; has been changed, but it has no way of knowing whether or not the change was mutually agreed upon. 6.1.4 . Eavesdropping and Data Integrity The iCalendar object is constructed with human-readable clear text. Any information contained in an iCalendar object may be read and/or changed by unauthorized persons while the object is in transit. 6.1.5 . Flooding a Calendar Implementations could provide a means to automatically incorporate &quot;REQUEST&quot; methods into a calendar. This presents the opportunity for a calendar to be flooded with requests, which effectively blocks all the calendar&#x27;s free time. 6.1.6 . Unauthorized REFRESH Requests It is possible for an &quot;Organizer&quot; to receive a &quot;REFRESH&quot; request from someone who is not an &quot;Attendee&quot; of an event or to-do. Only &quot;Attendees&quot; of an event or to-do are authorized to receive replies to &quot;REFRESH&quot; requests. Replying to such requests to anyone who is not an &quot;Attendee&quot; may be a security problem. 6.2 . Recommendations For an application where the information is sensitive or critical and the network is subject to a high probability of attack, iTIP transactions SHOULD be encrypted and authenticated. This helps mitigate the threats of spoofing, eavesdropping, and malicious changes in transit. 6.2.1 . Securing iTIP transactions iTIP transport bindings MUST provide a mechanism to enable authentication of the sender&#x27;s identity as well as privacy and integrity of the data being transmitted. This allows the receiver of a signed iCalendar object to verify the identity of the sender. This Daboo Standards Track [Page 125] RFC 5546 iTIP December 2009 sender may then be correlated to an &quot;ATTENDEE&quot; property in the iCalendar object. If the correlation is made and the sender is authorized to make the requested change or update, then the operation may proceed. It also allows the message to be encrypted to prevent unauthorized reading of the message contents in transit. iTIP transport binding documents describe this process in detail. 6.2.2 . Implementation Controls The threat of unauthorized replacement of the &quot;Organizer&quot; SHOULD be mitigated by a calendar system that uses this protocol by providing controls or alerts that make &quot;Calendar Users&quot; aware of such &quot;Organizer&quot; changes and allowing them to decide whether or not the request should be honored. The threat of flooding a calendar SHOULD be mitigated by a calendar system that uses this protocol by providing controls that may be used to limit the acceptable sources for iTIP transactions, and perhaps the size of messages and volume of traffic, by source. The threat of unauthorized &quot;REFRESH&quot; requests SHOULD be mitigated by a calendar system that uses this protocol by providing controls or alerts that allow &quot;Calendar Users&quot; to decide whether or not the request should be honored. An implementation MAY decide to maintain, for audit or historical purposes, &quot;Calendar Users&quot; who were part of an &quot;Attendee&quot; list and who were subsequently uninvited. Similar controls or alerts should be provided when a &quot;REFRESH&quot; request is received from these &quot;Calendar Users&quot; as well. 6.2.3 . Access Controls and Filtering In many environments, there could be restrictions on who is allowed to schedule with whom and who the allowed delegates are for particular &quot;Calendar Users&quot;. iTIP transport bindings SHOULD provide mechanisms for implementing access controls or filtering to ensure iTIP transactions only take place between authorized &quot;Calendar Users&quot;. That would include preventing one &quot;Calendar User&quot; from scheduling with another or one &quot;Calendar User&quot; delegating to another. 6.3 . Privacy Issues The &quot;Organizer&quot; might want to keep &quot;Attendees&quot; from knowing which other &quot;Attendees&quot; are participating in an event or to-do. The &quot;Organizer&quot; has the choice of sending single iTIP messages with a full list of &quot;Attendees&quot; or sending iTIP messages to each &quot;Attendee&quot; with only that &quot;Attendee&quot; listed.
