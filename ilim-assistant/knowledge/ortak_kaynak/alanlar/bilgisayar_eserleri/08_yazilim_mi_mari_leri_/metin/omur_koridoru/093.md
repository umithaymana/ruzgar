Forwarding an Event Request The protocol does not prevent an &quot;Attendee&quot; from &quot;forwarding&quot; a &quot;VEVENT&quot; calendar component to other &quot;Calendar Users&quot;. Forwarding differs from delegation in that the forwarded &quot;Calendar User&quot; (often referred to as a &quot;Party Crasher&quot;) does not replace the forwarding &quot;Calendar User&quot;. Implementations are not required to add the &quot;Party Crasher&quot; to the &quot;Attendee&quot; list, and hence there is no guarantee that a &quot;Party Crasher&quot; will receive additional updates to the event. The forwarding &quot;Calendar User&quot; SHOULD NOT add the &quot;Party Crasher&quot; to the &quot;Attendee&quot; list. The &quot;Organizer&quot; MAY add the forwarded &quot;Calendar User&quot; to the &quot;Attendee&quot; list. 4.2.9 . Replacing the Organizer The scenario for this example begins with &quot;A&quot; as the &quot;Organizer&quot; for a recurring meeting with &quot;B&quot;, &quot;C&quot;, and &quot;D&quot;. &quot;A&quot; receives a new job offer in another country and drops out of touch. &quot;A&quot; left no forwarding address or way to be reached. Using out-of-band communication, the other &quot;Attendees&quot; eventually learn what has happened and reach an agreement that &quot;B&quot; should become the new &quot;Organizer&quot; for the meeting. To do this, &quot;B&quot; sends out a new version of the event and the other &quot;Attendees&quot; agree to accept &quot;B&quot; as the new &quot;Organizer&quot;. &quot;B&quot; also removes &quot;A&quot; from the event. When the &quot;Organizer&quot; is replaced, the &quot;SEQUENCE&quot; property value MUST be incremented. This is the message &quot;B&quot; sends to &quot;C&quot; and &quot;D&quot;.
