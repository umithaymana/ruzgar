Array Concatenation and Splitting &#182; All of the preceding routines worked on single arrays. Concatenation of arrays &#182; Concatenation, or joining of two arrays in NumPy, is primarily accomplished using the routines np.concatenate , np.vstack , and np.hstack . np.concatenate takes a tuple or list of arrays as its first argument, as we can see here: In&nbsp;[43]: x = np . array ([ 1 , 2 , 3 ]) y = np . array ([ 3 , 2 , 1 ]) np . concatenate ([ x , y ]) Out[43]: array([1, 2, 3, 3, 2, 1]) You can also concatenate more than two arrays at once: In&nbsp;[44]: z = [ 99 , 99 , 99 ] print ( np . concatenate ([ x , y , z ])) [ 1 2 3 3 2 1 99 99 99] It can also be used for two-dimensional arrays: In&nbsp;[45]: grid = np . array ([[ 1 , 2 , 3 ], [ 4 , 5 , 6 ]]) In&nbsp;[46]: # concatenate along the first axis np . concatenate ([ grid , grid ]) Out[46]: array([[1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6], [1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6]]) In&nbsp;[47]: # concatenate along the second axis (zero-indexed) np . concatenate ([ grid , grid ], axis = 1 ) Out[47]: array([[1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6, 4, 5, 6]]) For working with arrays of mixed dimensions, it can be clearer to use the np.vstack (vertical stack) and np.hstack (horizontal stack) functions: In&nbsp;[48]: x = np . array ([ 1 , 2 , 3 ]) grid = np . array ([[ 9 , 8 , 7 ], [ 6 , 5 , 4 ]]) # vertically stack the arrays np . vstack ([ x , grid ]) Out[48]: array([[1, 2, 3], [9, 8, 7], [6, 5, 4]]) In&nbsp;[49]: # horizontally stack the arrays y = np . array ([[ 99 ], [ 99 ]]) np . hstack ([ grid , y ]) Out[49]: array([[ 9, 8, 7, 99], [ 6, 5, 4, 99]]) Similary, np.dstack will stack arrays along the third axis.
