Pursuing a degree in biology may lead to lucrative employment, or to unemployment and crushing debt. A doctor&rsquo;s appointment may result in the early detection and treatment of a disease, or it may be a waste of money. Expected utility theory is an account of how to choose rationally when you are not sure which outcome will result from your acts. Its basic slogan is: choose the act with the highest expected utility. This article discusses expected utility theory as a normative theory&mdash;that is, a theory of how people should make decisions. In classical economics, expected utility theory is often used as a descriptive theory&mdash;that is, a theory of how people do make decisions&mdash;or as a predictive theory&mdash;that is, a theory that, while it may not accurately model the psychological mechanisms of decision-making, correctly predicts people&rsquo;s choices. Expected utility theory makes faulty predictions about people&rsquo;s decisions in many real-life choice situations (see Kahneman &amp; Tversky 1982); however, this does not settle whether people should make decisions on the basis of expected utility considerations.
