Now we expand our description of motion to rotation—specifically, rotational motion about a fixed axis. We will find that rotational motion is described by a set of related variables similar to those we used in translational motion. Angular Velocity Uniform circular motion (discussed previously in Motion in Two and Three Dimensions ) is motion in a circle at constant speed. Although this is the simplest case of rotational motion, it is very useful for many situations, and we use it here to introduce rotational variables. In Figure 10.2 , we show a particle moving in a circle. The coordinate system is fixed and serves as a frame of reference to define the particle’s position. Its position vector from the origin of the circle to the particle sweeps out the angle θ θ , which increases in the counterclockwise direction as the particle moves along its circular path. The angle θ θ is called the angular position of the particle. As the particle moves in its circular path, it also traces an arc length s . The particle may complete more than one revolution around the circle, and so the angle θ θ may be greater than 2 π 2 π , and the arc length s may be greater than the circumference, 2 π r 2 π r .
