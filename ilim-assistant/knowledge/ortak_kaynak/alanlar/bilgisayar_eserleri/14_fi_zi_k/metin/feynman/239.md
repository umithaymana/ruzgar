Kinetic energy is a form of energy associated with the motion of a particle, single body, or system of objects moving together. We are aware that it takes energy to get an object, like a car or the package in Figure 7.4 , up to speed, but it may be a bit surprising that kinetic energy is proportional to speed squared. This proportionality means, for example, that a car traveling at 100 km/h has four times the kinetic energy it has at 50 km/h, helping to explain why high-speed collisions are so devastating. We will now consider a series of examples to illustrate various aspects of work and energy.
