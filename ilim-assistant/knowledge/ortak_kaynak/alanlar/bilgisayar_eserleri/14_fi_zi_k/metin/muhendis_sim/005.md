Damped, driven pendulum [ edit ] The above discussion focuses on a pendulum bob only acted upon by the force of gravity. Suppose a damping force, e.g. air resistance, as well as a sinusoidal driving force acts on the body. This system is a damped, driven oscillator , and is chaotic . Equation (1) can be written as m l 2 d 2 θ d t 2 = − m g l sin ⁡ θ (see the Torque derivation of Equation (1) above). A damping term and forcing term can be added to the right hand side to get m l 2 d 2 θ d t 2 = − m g l sin ⁡ θ − b d θ d t + a cos ⁡ ( Ω t ) where the damping is assumed to be directly proportional to the angular velocity (this is true for low-speed air resistance, see also Drag (physics) ). a and b are constants defining the amplitude of forcing and the degree of damping respectively. Ω is the angular frequency of the driving oscillations. Dividing through by m l 2 : d 2 θ d t 2 + b m l 2 d θ d t + g l sin ⁡ θ − a m l 2 cos ⁡ ( Ω t ) = 0. For a physical pendulum: d 2 θ d t 2 + b I d θ d t + m g r ⊕ I sin ⁡ θ − a I cos ⁡ ( Ω t ) = 0. This equation exhibits chaotic behaviour . The exact motion of this pendulum can only be found numerically and is highly dependent on initial conditions, e.g. the initial velocity and the starting amplitude. However, the small angle approximation outlined above can still be used under the required conditions to give an approximate analytical solution. Physical interpretation of the imaginary period [ edit ] The Jacobian elliptic function that expresses the position of a pendulum as a function of time is a doubly periodic function with a real period and an imaginary period. The real period is, of course, the time it takes the pendulum to go through one full cycle. Coupled pendula [ edit ] Two identical simple pendulums coupled via a spring connecting the bobs. Coupled pendulums can affect each other's motion, either through a direction connection (such as a spring connecting the bobs) or through motions in a supporting structure (such as a tabletop). The equations of motion for two identical simple pendulums coupled by a spring connecting the bobs can be obtained using Lagrangian mechanics . The kinetic energy of the system is: E K = 1 2 m L 2 ( θ ˙ 1 2 + θ ˙ 2 2 ) where m is the mass of the bobs, L is the length of the strings, and θ 1 , θ 2 are the angular displacements of the two bobs from equilibrium. The potential energy of the system is: E p = m g L ( 2 − cos ⁡ θ 1 − cos ⁡ θ 2 ) + 1 2 k L 2 ( θ 2 − θ 1 ) 2 where g is the gravitational acceleration , and k is the spring constant . The displacement L ( θ 2 − θ 1 ) of the spring from its equilibrium position assumes the small angle approximation .
