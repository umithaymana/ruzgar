Example 4.9 Entropy Change during Heat Transfer Heat flows from a steel object of mass 4.00 kg whose temperature is 400 K to an identical object at 300 K. Assuming that the objects are thermally isolated from the environment, what is the net entropy change of the universe after thermal equilibrium has been reached? Strategy Since the objects are identical, their common temperature at equilibrium is 350 K. To calculate the entropy changes associated with their transitions, we substitute the irreversible process of the heat transfer by two isobaric, reversible processes, one for each of the two objects. The entropy change for each object is then given by Δ S = m c ln ( T B / T A ) . Δ S = m c ln ( T B / T A ) . Solution Using c = 450 J/kg · K c = 450 J/kg · K , the specific heat of steel, we have for the hotter object Δ S h = ∫ T 1 T 2 m c d T T = m c ln T 2 T 1 = ( 4.00 kg ) ( 450 J/kg · K ) ln 350 K 400 K = −240 J/K . Δ S h = ∫ T 1 T 2 m c d T T = m c ln T 2 T 1 = ( 4.00 kg ) ( 450 J/kg · K ) ln 350 K 400 K = −240 J/K . Similarly, the entropy change of the cooler object is Δ S c = ( 4.00 kg ) ( 450 J/kg · K ) ln 350 K 300 K = 277 J/K . Δ S c = ( 4.00 kg ) ( 450 J/kg · K ) ln 350 K 300 K = 277 J/K . The net entropy change of the two objects during the heat transfer is then Δ S h + Δ S c = 37 J/K . Δ S h + Δ S c = 37 J/K . Significance The objects are thermally isolated from the environment, so its entropy must remain constant. Thus, the entropy of the universe also increases by 37 J/K.
