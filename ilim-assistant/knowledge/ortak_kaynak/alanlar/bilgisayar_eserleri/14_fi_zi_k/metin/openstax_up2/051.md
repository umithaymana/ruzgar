We do that in this section. First, we examine a process where the system has a constant volume, then contrast it with a system at constant pressure and show how their specific heats are related. Let’s start with looking at Figure 3.12 , which shows two vessels A and B , each containing the same type of ideal gas at a temperature T and a volume V . The only difference between the two vessels is that the piston at the top of A is fixed, whereas the one at the top of B is free to move against a constant external pressure p . We now consider what happens when the temperature of the gas in each vessel is slowly increased to T + d T T + d T with the addition of heat.
