Systems of Multiple Point Charges Just as the electric field obeys a superposition principle, so does the electric potential. Consider a system consisting of N charges q 1 , q 2 , … , q N . q 1 , q 2 , … , q N . What is the net electric potential V at a space point P from these charges? Each of these charges is a source charge that produces its own electric potential at point P , independent of whatever other changes may be doing. Let V 1 , V 2 , … , V N V 1 , V 2 , … , V N be the electric potentials at P produced by the charges q 1 , q 2 , … , q N , q 1 , q 2 , … , q N , respectively. Then, the net electric potential V P V P at that point is equal to the sum of these individual electric potentials. You can easily show this by calculating the potential energy of a test charge when you bring the test charge from the reference point at infinity to point P : V P = V 1 + V 2 + ⋯ + V N = ∑ 1 N V i . V P = V 1 + V 2 + ⋯ + V N = ∑ 1 N V i . Note that electric potential follows the same principle of superposition as electric field and electric potential energy.
