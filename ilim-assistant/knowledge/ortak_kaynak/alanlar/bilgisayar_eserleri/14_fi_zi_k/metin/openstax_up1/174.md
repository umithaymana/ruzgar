Example 13.2 Attraction between Galaxies Find the acceleration of our galaxy, the Milky Way , due to the nearest comparably sized galaxy, the Andromeda galaxy ( Figure 13.5 ). The approximate mass of each galaxy is 800 × 10 9 solar masses (a solar mass is the mass of our Sun), and they are separated by 2.5 million light-years. (Note that the mass of Andromeda is not so well known but is believed to be slightly larger than our galaxy.) Let’s estimate the time for this to happen. The initial acceleration is ~ 10 −13 m/s 2 ~ 10 −13 m/s 2 , so using v = a t v = a t , we see that it would take ~ 10 13 s ~ 10 13 s for each galaxy to reach a speed of 1.0 m/s, and they would be only ~ 0.5 × 10 13 m ~ 0.5 × 10 13 m closer. These two galaxies, along with about 50 other smaller galaxies, are all gravitationally bound into our local cluster.
