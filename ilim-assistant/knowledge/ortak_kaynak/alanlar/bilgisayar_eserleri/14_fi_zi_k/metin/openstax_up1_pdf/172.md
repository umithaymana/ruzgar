To simplify the situation we assume that the body acts as if its entire mass is concentrated at one specific point called the center of mass (CM). The concept of center of mass applies both to a single, extended object and to a system of objects that may be moving relative to each other. The center of mass is the average location of the mass if we divide the object (or system) into pieces of equal mass. The center of mass of a system of objects will respond to external forces as if all of the mass of the system were concentrated there.
