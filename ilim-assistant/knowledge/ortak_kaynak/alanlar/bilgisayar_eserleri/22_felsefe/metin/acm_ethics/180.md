While Locke gives an account of constraints on freedom that Berlin would call negative, he seems to endorse an account of MacCallum&rsquo;s third variable (the things one is free or unfree to do or become) that Berlin would call positive, restricting it to actions that are not immoral (liberty is not licence) and to those that are in the agent&rsquo;s own interests (I am not unfree if prevented from falling into a bog). Many contemporary liberals and libertarians have endorsed similarly morally-laden definitions of freedom (Nozick 1974; Rothbard 1982; Bader 2018). What these difficulties suggest is that the distinction between negative and positive liberty does not correspond to a single clear conceptual divide. But this is not to deny that the distinction continues to have a meaning. Positive and negative conceptions are best understood as stances taken up within intellectual traditions that have developed historically in response to distinct philosophical and political pressures (Garnett 2022b). In particular, the distinction functions as a rough categorization of different ideas of freedom in terms of the degree of critical distance implied between their proponents and the classical liberal tradition. One way of making sense of this degree of critical distance is by reference to the theorist&rsquo;s degree of concern with the notion of the self. Those on the positive side see questions about the nature and sources of a person&rsquo;s beliefs, desires, and values as relevant to questions about that person&rsquo;s freedom, whereas those on the negative side, being more faithful to the classical liberal tradition, tend to consider the raising of such questions as in some way indicating a propensity to violate the agent&rsquo;s dignity or integrity. One side takes a positive interest in the agent&rsquo;s beliefs, desires, and values, while the other recommends that we avoid doing so. This thought explains why the label &lsquo;pure negative freedom&rsquo; is an uncontroversial one, even though political philosophers diverge widely in how they assess the liberal normative theories that build on its value. Pure negative conceptions completely divorce questions about a person&rsquo;s freedom from questions about their will. Impure negative conceptions make some assumptions about typical or rational desires in order not to limit the relevant constraint-types to physical impossibility.
