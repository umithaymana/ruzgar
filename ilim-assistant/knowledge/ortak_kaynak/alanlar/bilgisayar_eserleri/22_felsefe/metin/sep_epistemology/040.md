# SEP — Epistemology (devam 40)

---

, Cuneo, and Shafer-Landau (2022) develop a different methodological framework on which philosophical theorizing is theory construction from data, where the data of philosophy are construed&mdash;per their &ldquo;Epistemic Theory of Data&rdquo;&mdash;not as agreed propositions, intuitive appearances, or facts about ordinary language, but as what inquirers have good epistemic reason to take to be genuine features of the relevant subject matter. Experimental results in epistemology raise metaepistemological questions. One such question concerns the extent to which facts about our patterns of using &ldquo;knows&rdquo; should inform epistemological theory (see &sect;3.2 ). Another related metaepistemological question of relevance here asks what the role of intuition is in epistemological theorizing; this bears relevance to the extent that experimental results purport to be testing folk (and expert) intuitions about cases. A third metaepistemological question experimental results in epistemology encourage concerns the comparative weight we should give to folk judgments about cases. A well-known position on this question&mdash;viz., the expertise reply &mdash;(e.g., Williamson 2011; Ludwig 2007; Kauppinen 2007) maintains that philosophers&rsquo; judgments about cases will plausibly be more reliable than those of laypersons, given that the former&rsquo;s training would lead them to be more sensitive to nuances of the cases assessed, as well as to be less likely to be less susceptible to irrelevant distorting effects (Egler &amp; Ross 2020: 1079). For criticism of the expertise defense, see e.g., Machery 2015 and Mizrahi 2015. Recent methodological work in the area includes Buckwalter&rsquo;s (2026) guide to thought-experiment design in epistemology, which diagnoses common confounds (naming effects, presentation order, etc.) and proposes remedies; Hamdo&rsquo;s (2023) book-length defense of the evidential role of intuition in epistemic thought experiments via a &ldquo;fittingness&rdquo; approach; and the proposal in Inarimori et al. (2026) that large language models might in principle generate &ldquo;artificial intuitions&rdquo; whose evidential role is distinct from, and in some ways less vulnerable to the framing and order effects affecting, human intuitions in experimental epistemology.
