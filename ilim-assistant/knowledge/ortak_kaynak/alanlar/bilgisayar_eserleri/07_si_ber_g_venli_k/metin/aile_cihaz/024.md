Franck Nijhof November 4, 2026 Core Release-Notes Comments Home Assistant 2026.11! 🎉 This is the biggest release we’ve ever shipped, by far. It’s ridiculously large. 🤯 A huge shout-out to everyone who helped make it happen, thank you! ❤️ For years, HACS, the Home Assistant Community Store, has been the place to find custom integrations, dashboard cards, and themes made by the community. Starting with this release, it’s built right into Home Assistant as the new Marketplace . I’ve been working on bringing it over myself, and my goal was simple: if you use HACS today, it should feel like only the name changed. Everything you installed stays installed and keeps getting updates. And if you never tried HACS because installing it felt like a hurdle, that hurdle is gone: it’s simply there, under Settings , on every installation type. 🛍️ None of this would exist without @ludeeus , who started HACS and donated it to the Open Home Foundation, and all the maintainers and developers who built that ecosystem over the years. 🙏 And this is just the first version of what a marketplace for Home Assistant could become. You know by now I’m an automation person, so I’m also very happy you can finally exclude items from a target . Turn on all the lights downstairs, except the one in the kitchen? Done. And it stays smart: add another light to the kitchen next month, and it’s left out too. 🧠 Then there’s naming. More than four years in the making, entity naming got a complete rethink: you name a thing once, and Home Assistant adds the context where it helps. Fair warning: this is a backward-incompatible change, and some names on your dashboards will look different after updating. The full story is in its own blog post . There’s more: Matter cameras you can talk back through , LoRaWAN support , sections that fill themselves , backups on local drives , a bunch of new ways to find out why something happened , and 38 new integrations! 🚀 Two more things before you dive in. November is the Month of “What the Heck?!” again, and this year it’s about everyone who lives in your home, including the people who didn’t set it up. Does your partner, kid, or housemate get annoyed by something in Home Assistant? Have them share it on the forum ! You can read all about it in the announcement . 🗣️ And this Saturday, November 7, is Community Day! There are 97 meetups around the world , in more than 30 countries, so there’s a good chance there’s one near you. This is your last chance to sign up. Thanks to them, these release notes are in great shape. ❤️ Welcome to the Marketplace For years, the community has built custom integrations A custom integration is an integration that has been created by someone from the Home Assistant community and has been published for others to use at their own risk. You can install custom integrations from the Marketplace. Custom integrations are not supported by the Home Assistant project. They are not reviewed or tested by the Home Assistant development team and thus may negatively impact the stability of your Home Assistant instance. An example of a custom integration is the Spook integration. [Learn more] , dashboard cards, themes, and templates for Home Assistant. People found them through HACS, the Home Assistant Community Store. With this release, HACS becomes part of Home Assistant as the new Marketplace . The Marketplace brings the existing HACS experience into Home Assistant and the regular release process. It works with every installation type, including Home Assistant Container Home Assistant Container is a standalone container-based installation of Home Assistant Core. Any OCI compatible runtime can be used, but the documentation focus is on Docker. [Learn more] , without manually downloading or unpacking files. Everything in the Marketplace is free, and that will not change. Browse and install You can find the Marketplace under Settings &gt; Marketplace . It comes with Home Assistant, so there is nothing to set up. As an administrator, you can browse the catalog and install integrations, dashboard cards, themes, and templates. You do not need a GitHub account to install or update projects. Installing a new integration Integrations connect and integrate Home Assistant with your devices, services, and more. [Learn more] got shorter, too. In HACS, you downloaded it, restarted Home Assistant, went to Devices &amp; services , and searched for it again to set it up. In the Marketplace, you select Install , and you land straight in its setup flow, without a restart. Updates still need a restart for now. The page of each project now shows more than its README: how often it was downloaded, its stars on GitHub, and the Home Assistant version it requires, so you know what you’re getting into before you install. Every project you install gets an “update entity” An update entity is an entity that indicates if an update is available for a device or service. [Learn more] , so updates show up under Settings &gt; Updates , next to the updates of Home Assistant itself. After an update, a repair tells you when a restart is needed, and can restart Home Assistant for you. The first time you open the Marketplace, it shows a notice. Everything in the Marketplace is made by the community. It is not tested, audited, or supported by the Home Assistant project, and it runs inside your Home Assistant. Read the notice, select I understand the risks and agree , and you’re in. Browsing works before that. Installing and updating does not. The Marketplace adds several improvements: Group the Browse and Installed lists by status, type, or source. Home Assistant remembers your grouping. Filter by source to distinguish the community catalog from custom repositories. Select multiple repositories on the Installed tab and uninstall them at once. Skipped updates no longer keep a repository at the top of the list or count in the update badge. A new release makes the update available again. Search, filters, and sorting are saved in the URL, so you can share a link to a specific Marketplace view. Integrations show their brand images before you install them. Custom repositories Not every project is in the catalog. To add one that is missing, select Add from link and enter its repository URL. The Marketplace then lists it with your other repositories. The Custom repositories page lists repositories you added this way and lets you open or remove them. A repository stays in the list until you uninstall it. Custom repositories require a GitHub connection. The Marketplace uses GitHub device sign-in, so you approve the connection on GitHub without entering your password in Home Assistant. The connection only reads public information and cannot access private repositories. Where your downloads come from By default, the Marketplace gets its catalog and everything you install from GitHub. Where GitHub cannot be reached, such as in mainland China, you can switch to the mirrors run by the Home Assistant China community. Go to Settings &gt; Marketplace , select the three dots menu in the upper-right corner, and select Download source .
