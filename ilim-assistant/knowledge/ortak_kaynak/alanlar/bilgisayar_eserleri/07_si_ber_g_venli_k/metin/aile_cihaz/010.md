[ 113 ] Section 8 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms protects against unreasonable search or seizure and has been interpreted to protect reasonable expectations of privacy. [ 114 ] In Jones v Tsige (2012) the Court of Appeal for Ontario recognised a common-law tort of intrusion upon seclusion. [ 115 ] In Quebec, privacy is protected by articles 3 and 35 to 41 of the Civil Code of Quebec and by section 5 of the Quebec Charter of Human Rights and Freedoms . [ 116 ] [ 117 ] China [ edit ] Further information: Personal Information Protection Law of the People's Republic of China The Civil Code of the People's Republic of China , in force since 1 January 2021, recognises a right to privacy ( yinsi quan ) and defines privacy as "the undisturbed private life of a natural person and his private space, private activities and private information that he does not want to be known to others" (article 1032), alongside separate provisions on the protection of personal information (articles 1034–1039). [ 118 ] The Personal Information Protection Law , in force since 1 November 2021, establishes a comprehensive regime for the processing of personal information by both private and state bodies. [ 119 ] European Union [ edit ] Main article: General Data Protection Regulation The General Data Protection Regulation, adopted in 2016 and applicable from 25 May 2018, applies to the processing of personal data by public and private bodies within the Union and to organisations outside it that offer goods or services to, or monitor, people in the Union. [ 120 ] Processing is lawful only on one of six legal bases, of which consent is one; the others include the performance of a contract, compliance with a legal obligation and the "legitimate interests" of the controller. [ 121 ] The regulation grants data subjects rights of access, rectification, erasure and objection, and allows fines of up to €20 million or four per cent of worldwide annual turnover. [ 122 ] In 2023 the Irish Data Protection Commission fined Meta €1.2 billion for transferring European users' data to the United States. [ 123 ] The Court of Justice of the European Union has given these rights considerable force. In Google Spain (2014) it held that a search engine could be required to delist results about a person in certain circumstances, a ruling popularly described as establishing a " right to be forgotten ". [ 124 ] In Digital Rights Ireland (2014) it invalidated the Data Retention Directive, and in Schrems II (2020) it annulled the EU–US "Privacy Shield" arrangement because of the breadth of American surveillance law. [ 125 ] [ 126 ] Hong Kong [ edit ] Main article: Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance Article 30 of the Basic Law provides that "the freedom and privacy of communication of Hong Kong residents shall be protected by law", and article 14 of the Hong Kong Bill of Rights reproduces article 17 of the ICCPR. [ 127 ] The Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486), enacted in 1995 and in force in its main part since 20 December 1996, was one of the first comprehensive data protection laws in Asia and is enforced by the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data . [ 128 ] [ 129 ] Amendments in 2021 created offences directed at " doxxing ". [ 128 ] In Koo Sze Yiu v Chief Executive (2006), the Court of Final Appeal upheld declarations that the legal basis for covert surveillance by law-enforcement agencies was unconstitutional, suspending them for six months to allow legislation; the Interception of Communications and Surveillance Ordinance (Cap. 589) was enacted later that year. [ 127 ] The Law Reform Commission of Hong Kong recommended a statutory tort of invasion of privacy in 2004, but no such tort has been enacted. [ 130 ] India [ edit ] Further information: Aadhaar In Justice K. S. Puttaswamy v Union of India (2017), a nine-judge bench of the Supreme Court of India unanimously held that privacy is a fundamental right protected by Article 21 and Part III of the Constitution, overruling earlier decisions to the contrary. [ 131 ] The case arose from challenges to Aadhaar , the biometric identification scheme launched in 2009. In a separate judgment in 2018, a five-judge bench upheld the scheme by a majority of four to one, but struck down the provision that had permitted private companies to use Aadhaar for authentication. [ 132 ] The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 introduced India's first comprehensive data protection statute. [ 133 ] United Kingdom [ edit ] Main article: Privacy in English law English law long declined to recognise a general tort of invasion of privacy, a position the House of Lords reaffirmed in Wainwright v Home Office (2003). [ 134 ] After the Human Rights Act 1998 gave domestic effect to Article 8 of the European Convention, however, the courts developed the equitable action for breach of confidence into a distinct cause of action for misuse of private information . In Campbell v MGN Ltd (2004) the House of Lords asked whether the claimant had a reasonable expectation of privacy and, if so, balanced that interest against freedom of expression; [ 135 ] in Vidal-Hall v Google (2015) the Court of Appeal held that misuse of private information is a tort. [ 136 ] Data protection is governed by the Data Protection Act 2018 and the "UK GDPR" retained after Brexit, and is overseen by the Information Commissioner's Office . [ 137 ] United States [ edit ] Main article: Privacy laws of the United States The United States Constitution does not mention privacy. The Fourth Amendment protects "the right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, and effects, against unreasonable searches and seizures", which the Supreme Court in Katz v. United States (1967) extended to situations in which a person has a "reasonable expectation of privacy". [ 138 ] In Griswold v. Connecticut (1965) the Court found a right of marital privacy in the "penumbras" of several guarantees of the Bill of Rights , [ 47 ] and later decisions, including Roe v. Wade (1973), located a right of privacy in the liberty protected by the Due Process Clause . Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization (2022) overruled Roe . The majority stated that its decision did not cast doubt on precedents not concerning abortion, but Justice Clarence Thomas , concurring, urged the Court to reconsider its substantive due process precedents, including Griswold . [ 139 ] There is no comprehensive federal data protection statute. The Privacy Act of 1974 applies only to federal agencies, and private-sector data is regulated by sector-specific laws such as the Fair Credit Reporting Act (1970), the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (1996), the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (1998) and the Gramm–Leach–Bliley Act (1999), and by the Federal Trade Commission 's powers over unfair and deceptive practices. [ 11 ] [ 7 ] In 2019 the Commission imposed a penalty of US$5 billion on Facebook for violating an earlier privacy order. [ 140 ] Several states have enacted general consumer privacy laws, beginning with the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018. [ 141 ] In a report on his 2017 visit, published in 2021, the UN Special Rapporteur on the right to privacy, Joseph Cannataci, recommended that safeguards and remedies for foreign intelligence be raised to the standard applying to domestic intelligence, stating that he did not accept the approach of any country that applies lower privacy protection to non-nationals. [ 142 ] Privacy harms [ edit ] See also: Expectation of privacy In 1960 William Prosser argued that American privacy case law in fact comprised four distinct torts: [ 143 ] intrusion upon a person's seclusion or solitude, or into his private affairs; public disclosure of embarrassing private facts; publicity that places a person in a false light in the public eye; and appropriation, for the defendant's advantage, of the person's name or likeness. Courts in most American states continue to recognise these four torts. [ 11 ] Building on Prosser's scheme, Solove proposed a broader taxonomy of sixteen kinds of activity that cause privacy problems, grouped into four categories: [ 144 ] [ 145 ] Information collection : surveillance and interrogation . [ 146 ] Information processing : aggregation , identification, insecurity, secondary use (use of data for purposes other than those for which it was collected without the subject's consent) and exclusion (failure to let people know about or participate in the handling of their data). [ 147 ] Information dissemination : breach of confidentiality, disclosure, exposure, increased accessibility, blackmail , appropriation and distortion. [ 148 ] Invasion : intrusion and decisional interference, that is, governmental interference with a person's decisions about private matters. [ 149 ] Solove, building from Prosser and other historical precedents, argues that a taxonomy of problems is more useful to law and policy than an abstract definition, because it identifies the specific activities that cause harm and the distinct interests they affect.
