ECON 159 - Lecture 15 - Backward Induction: Chess, Strategies, and Credible Threats | Open Yale Courses Skip to main content Yale University Menu Search Open Yale Courses ECON 159: Game Theory Lecture 15 - Backward Induction: Chess, Strategies, and Credible Threats Overview We first discuss Zermelo's theorem: that games like tic-tac-toe or chess have a solution. That is, either there is a way for player 1 to force a win, or there is a way for player 1 to force a tie, or there is a way for player 2 to force a win. The proof is by induction. Then we formally define and informally discuss both perfect information and strategies in such games. This allows us to find Nash equilibria in sequential games. But we find that some Nash equilibria are inconsistent with backward induction. In particular, we discuss an example that involves a threat that is believed in an equilibrium but does not seem credible.
