Automation conditions - Home Assistant 2026.11.1 On this page Built-in and integration conditions Conditions check the current state Conditions and building blocks Disabling a condition Testing conditions Related topics Home ▸ Documentation ▸ Automation Automation conditions A condition checks whether something is true right now. For example, a condition can check whether a door is closed, or whether someone is home. When a trigger starts the automation, Home Assistant checks the conditions. If they are met, the automation runs its actions . If not, the automation stops. You add conditions in the And if section of the automation editor, with Add condition . Conditions are optional. Without conditions, the automation runs its actions whenever a trigger starts it. Built-in and integration conditions Conditions that come with an integration are named after what they check. Integration conditions include Light is on and Sun is up . Conditions that are built into Home Assistant aren’t tied to an integration. You can use them with entities from any integration. Built-in conditions include State , Numeric state , Time , and Template . For all conditions, refer to the list of available conditions . The built-in conditions are in the General group. The Device condition is in the Device automation group. Many conditions check a target, such as a light or all windows in an area. If the target covers several entities, the Condition passes if option decides whether one or all of them must match. For details, refer to Automation targets . To leave out some of the targets, refer to Excluding targets . Conditions check the current state A condition checks the state at the moment the automation runs. This is why: A condition doesn’t start the automation when it becomes true. To start the automation when something becomes true, use a trigger. If the state changes after the check, the condition isn’t checked again. The automation continues or stops based on that one check. For examples, refer to Conditions check the current state . Conditions and building blocks If an automation has several conditions, all of them must be met. To combine conditions in a different way, select Add condition , and then select Blocks : Or : met if at least one of the conditions inside is met. Not : met if none of the conditions inside are met. And : met if all conditions inside are met. Use it inside an Or or Not block. You can also check a condition between the actions under Then do : To stop the remaining actions when a condition isn’t met, use the Condition building block. To do different things depending on a condition, use If-then or Choose . Disabling a condition To try an automation without one of its conditions, disable the condition instead of deleting it. In the editor, in the Menu of the condition, select Disable . For details, refer to Turning off a trigger, condition, or action . A disabled condition is skipped, as if it isn’t there. In YAML, add enabled: false to the condition. enabled can also be a limited template or a blueprint input. The limited template can’t use variables, such as trigger , this , or values from the variables section. If the limited template refers to a variable, the condition is disabled or causes an error. For details, refer to Variables in limited templates . For conditions in the And if section, Home Assistant checks the value once, when the automation loads. A condition that is a step of its own in the actions works like any other step: its enabled option can be a regular template that uses states and variables. For details, refer to Turning off a step . Testing conditions You can check whether a condition is met right now, without running the automation. For the steps, refer to Testing a condition .
